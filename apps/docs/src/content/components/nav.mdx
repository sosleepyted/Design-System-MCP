`Nav` is the page header: a wordmark, primary links, an optional call to action,
and a slot for actions like a locale toggle. Below the medium breakpoint the
links collapse into a menu opened from the `menu` icon. One component serves both
surfaces through the `mode` prop, so the brand site and the internal app stay in
step.

## When to use

**Use Nav:**
- as the top-level navigation on any brand or product page
- when you need a built-in mobile menu rather than a hand-rolled one

**Don't use Nav:**
- for in-page section links (use anchors in the content)
- for a sidebar or secondary navigation

### Mobile menu

At narrow widths the links and CTA collapse behind the `menu` icon and open in a
panel that slides down. Resize the demo below 768px to try it.

## Behaviour

### Interaction

The menu button toggles the panel and swaps the `menu` icon for `close`. It
carries `aria-expanded` and points at the panel with `aria-controls`. The panel
uses the shared slide-down keyframe, which is switched off under reduced motion.
Selecting a link closes the panel.

### Placement

`Nav` is sticky to the top of the viewport. Keep it as the first element on the
page.

## Accessibility

- Renders a `<header>` with a labelled `<nav>` landmark.
- The menu button announces its state with `aria-expanded` and is labelled
  bilingually (<Icon name="menu" /> opens, <Icon name="close" /> closes).
- Links and the menu button meet the 36px minimum touch target.
- Focus rings follow the navy focus token.

## Content

- Link labels start with a noun, stay to one or two words, and are bilingual
  (DE and EN) via the locale layer.
- The CTA starts with a verb. Sentence case, no full stop, no em dashes.
- Allow about 2x growth when a label is translated.

| <Icon name="check_circle" className="text-[#0f5e2a]" /> Do | <Icon name="close" className="text-[#a32d2d]" /> Don't |
|---|---|
| "Komponenten" | "Hier geht es zu den Komponenten" |
| "Personal anfragen" | "Jetzt sofort Personal anfragen!" |
