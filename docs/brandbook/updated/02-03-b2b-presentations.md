# B2B Presentation Guide

Everything needed to build and deliver a ucm.agency B2B presentation lives on
this page: assets, design rules, the pitch structure, content depth by deck
size, and tone. Decks are external-facing surfaces, so they follow brand
mode: the UCM triad, Figtree, and a restrained yellow accent.

## Who these decks are for

- Clients: companies across industries with staffing needs, for example event
  agencies, airports, duty-free shops, and trade fairs.
- Project size: from small short-term campaigns to large long-term projects
  with several hundred workers.

## Core principles

- Show, do not explain. Use images instead of text blocks.
- One statement per slide.
- Remove everything unnecessary. Focus on the message.
- Speak with the audience, not at it.
- Use storytelling: opening, conflict, resolution.
- Prepare well, but stay open to the moment.
- Your presence counts for more than your slides.

## Design rules

- No more than six words per slide.
- Font size: at least 30 pt.
- Use strong, relevant images from the photo gallery above.
- No clipart, no emoji, no standard template layouts. If a slide needs an
  icon, use the Material Symbols set, matching the product's Icon component.
- Typeface: Figtree, with headings in Light 300 and tight tracking. Nunito
  appears only inside the logo asset. Do not add other typefaces.
- Contrast comes from the brand pairings, never from pure black or white:
  - Light slides: cream #F5F5F3 background with navy #001E2B text.
  - Dark slides: near-black navy #0A0F14 background with off-cream #F0F0EB
    text. Use dark slides sparingly, like the cinematic dark hero on the
    website.
  - Yellow #FCC224 is the accent and stays scarce, at most about 10 percent
    of any slide.
- White space is not wasted space.

## The pitch spine

Every deck, whatever its size, follows the same arc. Start with the client's
problem, never with ucm.agency talking about itself.

1. Problem: what exactly is at stake for the client?
2. Solution: what do we offer?
3. Benefit: what does the client get out of it?
4. Proof: why should they believe us? The company introduction lives here,
   as evidence, not as the opening.
5. Call to action: what should the client do now?

## Content depth by deck size

The spine stays fixed; the deck size decides how deep each step goes.

### Small (flyer, one-pager, sales pitch)

Goal: short and to the point, to spark interest and start a first
conversation.

- Title and claim: a clear, catchy message that addresses the client's need.
  German example: "Flexibles Personal für Ihre Veranstaltungen. Mit
  ucm.agency zum Erfolg."
- Problem: the staffing pain in one line (short-notice gaps, peak loads,
  quality risk).
- Solution:
  - Flexible staff for short-term assignments
  - Tailored staffing solutions for events, promotions, and trade fairs
  - Quality assured through training and on-site management
  - Fast placement through large talent pools and automated backup systems
- Benefit: flexibility, speed, high quality, tailored to the client.
- Proof: one-line company introduction (founding year, talent pool,
  specialization in airport, trade fair, or event staff) plus references such
  as Messe Berlin, Dolce & Gabbana, and Deutsche Bahn.
- Call to action: invite the client to get in touch or book a meeting.

### Medium (standard pitch decks)

Goal: a more detailed pitch focused on solutions for the client's specific
needs.

- Title and claim: a precise statement of the offer. German example: "Mit
  ucm.agency Ihr Event perfekt besetzen. Zuverlässigkeit und Flexibilität
  garantiert."
- Problem: the client's industry-specific staffing challenge, stated in
  their terms.
- Solution, in detail:
  - Staffing for all event and trade fair formats
  - Tailored recruiting processes and audience-specific outreach
  - Digital tools for fast booking and administration
  - On-site management and continuous performance optimization
  - How we deliver: the recruiting process from posting to final placement,
    on-site project management, and a backup plan with automated re-staffing
- Benefit: the measurable outcomes the client cares about, tied to their
  needs.
- Proof: a short company introduction (three to four sentences, specialized
  for the client's industry) plus detailed case studies with results
  (revenue growth, conversion rates, client satisfaction).
- Call to action: invite a deeper conversation or a workshop.

### Large (full pitch decks for major projects)

Goal: a complete picture of the offer, with a detailed solution for one
specific major project.

- Title and claim: a clear, persuasive statement of the value for the
  client. German example: "Maßgeschneiderte Personallösungen für Ihre
  Großprojekte. Mit ucm.agency alles aus einer Hand."
- Problem: the specific major project and what is at stake, from first
  inquiry to completion.
- Solution, comprehensive:
  - Individually tailored staffing solutions for long-term major projects
  - Extensive experience with large events (trade fairs, festivals, airports)
  - IT tools and apps for efficient staff management
  - Full on-site support with supervisors on location
  - Project plan: phases (concept development, recruiting, training, on-site
    management), timeline, and milestones
  - Risk and incident management: automated backup, AI-driven absence
    forecasting, continuous support
  - Cost structure: transparent costs for staff, training, equipment,
    travel, and other items
- Benefit: the KPIs the project will move, for example ROI, cost
  optimization, staff availability, and performance per shift.
- Proof: detailed company facts (revenue, number of projects, team size),
  the specialization in large events, airports, and luxury brands, full case
  studies with concrete numbers, and the project team with its points of
  contact, expertise, and responsibilities.
- Call to action: invite contract signing or a closing meeting.

## Dos and don'ts

Do:

- Use personal, relevant stories.
- Use pauses deliberately.
- Start with a strong question or thesis.
- Use simple transitions between topics. If a slide animates at all, keep it
  subtle and ease-out, in line with the brand motion rules.

Don't:

- No long bullet lists.
- No complex tables.
- No sound effects and no transition animations.
- No full sentences on slides.

## Language and tone

- Direct and clear, no detours. Use active phrasing.
- No buzzwords, no empty phrases.
- Focus on the client's benefit, not on self-praise.
- Adjust the register to the deck size, from professional and factual for
  major projects to friendly and inviting for smaller ones.
- Decks shown to German-speaking clients are written in German, others in
  English. Either way the copy rules hold: no em dashes, no emoji, no
  uppercase kickers.
- Tailor every deck to the specific client; focus on the solutions that
  cover exactly their requirements.
